Smart motion sensors are cheap compared with cameras, but they can make a smart home feel far more useful. They turn lights on, trigger alerts, start routines, and help an alarm system know when something is moving indoors.

The key is choosing the right type. A basic PIR motion sensor is fine for halls and lights. A presence sensor is better for living rooms and offices where you sit still. A contact sensor is better for doors and windows.

## Sensor Types Compared

| Sensor type | Best for | Avoid using it for |
|---|---|---|
| PIR motion sensor | Halls, stairs, cupboards, quick light triggers | Rooms where people sit still for long periods |
| Presence sensor | Offices, living rooms, kitchens, occupancy-based automations | Simple low-cost security alerts |
| Contact sensor | Doors, windows, cupboards, medicine cabinets | Detecting people moving around a room |
| Alarm motion detector | Security systems and away modes | Fine-grained lighting automations |

## Where To Put Smart Sensors

Good locations:

- Hallways.
- Stairs and landings.
- Utility rooms.
- Home offices.
- Kitchens.
- Cupboards and garages.
- Back doors and patio doors.

Weak locations:

- Facing radiators or direct sunlight.
- Behind furniture.
- Too close to curtains.
- Where pets constantly trigger them.
- Outside, unless the sensor is rated for it.

## Final Recommendation

For lights, buy the sensor that matches your lighting ecosystem: **Philips Hue Motion Sensor** for Hue, **Aqara P1** for Zigbee/Home Assistant, and **Eve Motion** for Matter/Thread.
